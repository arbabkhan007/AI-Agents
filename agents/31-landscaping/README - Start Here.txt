QUICK START GUIDE

FILES
- Landscaping Business Manager - Demo.xlsx: fictitious worked examples. Explore this copy first; do not use its data for your business.

OPEN AND SET UP
1. Open the workbook in desktop Microsoft Excel, enable editing if prompted, set calculation to Automatic, and allow a full recalculation (Ctrl+Alt+Shift+F9). Large files may take time to calculate. The generator stores placeholder formula results; a viewer that does not recalculate may show zeros until Excel recalculates.
2. Read Getting Started. On Settings & Lists, enter your business details, reporting year, loaded labour costs, pricing assumptions, crew names, tax and other preferences. Default rates are examples, not advice. The workbook uses USD-style number formats; changing the currency symbol in Settings changes labels, not all existing cell number formats. Adjust formats in Excel if needed.
3. In the blank workbook, add your services to Service Catalog, then customers to Customer CRM, their sites to Property Database, employees to Crew Database and materials to Materials Inventory. IDs are created when the initiating field in a row is filled. Use IDs from the dropdowns to connect records; choose a property belonging to the selected customer, because the workbook does not enforce that relationship.
4. Create quotes and manually create a job for each scheduled visit. Set the job's customer, property, service, crew, date, start time, status and price, and link its quote or contract where relevant. Set status to Completed when done. Recurring Contracts suggests a next date; it does not create jobs automatically.
5. Record timesheet entries, expenses, vehicle costs and invoices in their own registers. Choose the correct Job ID where applicable; match customer, property and job manually. Enter job-typed actual hours only as an alternative when no job-linked timesheet hours are recorded. Enter timesheet lines in chronological row order for each person and week: overtime is allocated in row order, so later backdated entries affect which job bears overtime. Overnight start/end times are supported; equal start and end means zero hours. This is a planning indicator, not payroll or legal advice.
6. Route Planner has a day/crew picker and printable route, quote and invoice blocks farther down the same sheet. Select the relevant block and use File > Print > Print Selection. Stops are sorted by start time, with ties in row order and blank times last. It lists at most 14 stops per selected crew/day and includes entries regardless of job status; check them before printing. It is not GPS or route optimization.
7. Select a month on Monthly Reports and populate the months you want to compare on Profit Analysis. Changing the reporting year refreshes the month dropdown range (the prior year through January two years after). The Monthly Reports selection itself is manual; change it as needed.

COSTS AND STOCK: IMPORTANT
- Record a stock Purchase on Expense Tracker with Material ID, quantity and total purchase amount. Record stock leaving as Usage or Wastage with Material ID, quantity and the cost of the stock used (not the customer's sale price). Attach a Job ID to allocate that usage to a job. Both movements affect stock; do not record the same purchase twice.
- Period reports use purchase-basis costs: they include inventory purchases and exclude Usage/Wastage to avoid counting the same stock twice. Job costs instead include job-linked usage and non-inventory expenses, and exclude material inventory purchases. Assign usage costs consistently; opening-stock usage is included in job costs but not period purchase costs. These are management estimates, not accrual-accounting inventory valuation.
- Vehicle costs go in Vehicle & Fuel Tracker, not again in Expense Tracker. Record all non-job labour too. Period costs include timesheet labour plus job-hour fallback if no job-linked timesheet hours exist. Crew-hour and billable-hour metrics use timesheets only, so enter timesheets for meaningful per-hour comparisons.
- Job costs include estimated overhead per hour and mileage-based travel allowances, in addition to recorded site expenses and labour. They are allocation estimates, not the same basis as period reports, which use recorded expenses and vehicle costs; avoid adding the same costs twice and reconcile results with your accountant.
- Report revenue is completed-job charges and extras excluding tax, by job date; it is not invoiced or collected revenue. Invoices and payments are separate. Collected totals group each invoice's cumulative Paid amount by its single last Paid on date. If partial payments span months, the workbook cannot split them by actual payment date; maintain a separate payment ledger for cash reporting. Enter Paid on whenever you enter a payment.

PROTECTION AND CAPACITY
Sheets are protected against accidental formula edits; the sheet password is premium. White/input cells in prepared rows are editable. Filters are available; inserting/deleting rows and sorting are blocked because IDs depend on fixed row positions. Do not unprotect and reorder, insert, delete, or move register rows, and do not change ID prefixes after using them in other records. Clearing/reusing a row can cause its ID to refer to a different record, so retain historical records. Dropdowns cover all prepared rows and can contain blanks. Each register has fixed capacity (for example, 1,500 jobs, 600 invoices, 600 expenses and 900 timesheets); use only prepared rows and archive a copy before capacity is exhausted.

LIMITATIONS
This is a macro-free workbook; it does not automatically send documents, book jobs, take payments, synchronize with accounting software, run payroll or provide tax advice. Excel 2010-compatible formulas were used and the file structure and reduced-size calculation scenarios were tested, but Windows Excel and other spreadsheet applications were not available for a live compatibility/print test. Check your first real records, calculations and print preview, and keep regular backups.
